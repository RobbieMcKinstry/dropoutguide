# Robbie's 2025 Dropout TV Guide

By Robbie McKinstry. Last updated 12/20/24

Dropout is an independent online streaming service. At a time when most Americans are suffering subscription fatigue from too many useless streaming sites, Dropout offers a fresh, affordable departure from the fragmented corporate streaming ecosystem. First, Dropout doesn't syndicate content from other networks. Almost everything on Dropout is media developed, produced, and owned by Dropout. Second, there are very few shows in active development. Dropout had about six shows in production in 2023, all of which are worth watching. Despite this tiny number of originals, each is spectacular in its own right. In 2024, the number of Dropout originals is set to grow, and the first of which, Very Important People, has started to be released. Finally, most of the content is improv. At the risk of generalizing, Hollywood doesn't believe improv comedy can have a high enough bar to maintain an audience. This is Dropout's biggest innovation: the regular cast who appear on the various Dropout improv shows are consistently funny and demonstrate that, indeed, improv content can have a high bar.

This guide describes some of my favorite Dropout shows and includes recommended episodes to whet your appetite and give you a feel for the show's vibe.

## Game Changer

![game-changer](/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/game-changer.png)

[Hailed by Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/23639574/dropout-tv-college-humor-game-changer-sam-reich) as "one of the funniest, nicest, cleverest pieces of TV," Game Changer is a game show with an ever-changing ruleset. The game's rules are different in every episode, and the contestants are in the dark until they start gaining or losing points. Sam Reich, the CEO of Dropout, writes, directs, and hosts this unbelievable show.

* **"A Game Most Changed," Season 5, Episode 5:** A tour de force of improv, where an entire Shakespearian-style play is improvised with added constraints worth additional points.
* **"Beat the Buzzer" Season 6 Episode 7:** A simple trivia game spirals out of control once the contestants realize its getting harder and harder to buzz in. 
* **"Do I Hear $1?" Season 2 Episode 2:**  How low would you go? The contestants are given the chance to take home prize money, but this episode is a race to the bottom.
* **"Escape the Greenroom," Season 5, Episode 9:** The contestants don't even make it to the stage before the game begins! These three D&D experts find themselves in a real-life puzzle room when they realize they've been locked in an escape room.
* **"Bingo" Season 6 Episode 5:** The contestents are playing bingo, but Sam is playing 4D chess. Giving any concrete information about this mindfuck episode would spoil the conceit. 
* **"Whodunnit": Season 1 Episode 4:** There's a murder on set! The contestants have to ascertain whodunnit, Clue-style, by interviewing the crew.
* **"Game Changer: Survivor," Season 4 Episode 9:** This two-part episode captures the palatable excitement at the core of every Game Changer episode.
* **"Sam Says 3" Season 6 Episode ?**: Building on two previous episodes worth of stakes-raising, this episode takes Simon Says to the extreme. You can watch this episode without having seen the other two.

## Breaking News

![breaking news](/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/breaking news.jpg)

Laughter is contagious in this show, where four actors must read a surprise script from a teleprompter without laughing. Whoever laughs loses a point, and whoever has the lowest score at the end of the episode loses.

* **"Gengar could run a train through me," Season 5 Episode 10**
* **"Dropout America 2: Season 6, Episode 1:"** When you can make your company's CEO say whatever you want, how far do you take it?
* **"True Facts about Grant Anthony O'Brien (Part 2)," Season 5 Episode 9:** This episode departs from the normal format and covers true facts instead of made-up news stories.
* **"Bummer Tornado Destroys Cool Jail":** The first episode of many in which Amy Vorpahl can't hold it together.
* **"Gilchrest Savoy, the Wealthiest Man of 1906:" Season 5, Episode 11.** Trapp has to improv a gentlemanly time-traveler.

## Dimension 20

<img src="/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/dimension-20.webp" alt="dimension-20" style="zoom:150%;" />

*Dimension 20* is a flagship show of Dropout and a favorite with "Actual Play" D&D fans. It's largely considered the #2 D&D Actual Play show, second only to Critical Role. It's a much slower burn than the other shows, and it might not be your cup of tea if you're not into podcasts.

There are a ton of *Dimension 20* campaigns, and they're all quite different. You can consult [this chart](https://dimension20.fandom.com/wiki/Watch_Order#By_Tone_and_Genre) for a breakdown by genre and tone. 

* I recommend *Misfits and Magic*, a micro-campaign that serves as a criticism of Harry Potter by a group that deeply loves Harry Potter. 
* I really love Fantasy High, Season 1, which is the first season of Dimension 20. It gets started more quickly than other seasons and has a number of iconic moments and characters. Fantasy High is the only season of Dimension 20 with three sequels: Sophmore Year, Junior Year, and The Seven (a spin-off).
* *The Unsleeping City* is the second season of Dimension 20. It's an unban fantasy making place in the city that never sleeps. As a longtime D&D player, I was really skeptical of an urban fantasy setting, but the Unsleeping City really delivers. 

* I chose to watch *Escape from the Blood Keep* first because Brennen Lee Mulligan is an excellent DM, and Matt Mercer of Critical Role fame is a player. 

* Fans of drag may like "Dungeons and Drag Queens," especially if you're new to D&D.
* If you like Hank Green, you can watch him in Mentopolis, which is something of noir detective cross between *Osmosis Jones* and *Inside Out*.

## Gastronauts

Gastronauts is a show for the all the Food Network afficianados out there – a side-splitting pastiche to chef challenge shows like *Chopped* and Beat *Bobby Flay*, this show delivers on creative, funny challenges while still managing to make every dish look delicatable. I expect Gastronauts to be renewed without much hesitation. This season had a fairly short order due to its larger budget relative to other shows on Dropout.

## Smartypants

In the Smartypants Society, intellectual hangers-on present comical (though sometimes, accidentally educational) presentations on a topic of interest or thought experiment. A Powerpoint Party with the vibe of a French salon. Each talk is between five and ten minutes, with three per episode on average. Reka Shankar hosts, whom Axios has called an ["up-and-coming comedian you should know."](https://www.axios.com/local/philadelphia/2024/12/20/rekha-shankar-smartypants-philly-comedian)

* **"Swing Music, Emails, The Moon" Season 1 Episode 3:** Ross Bryant, a Swing Music Accelerationist, delivers a Shakespearan oration on the inevidability return of swing music to save culture. I defy you to find a better lightning talk anywhere on the internet.
* **"Birthdays, The Cookout, Vegetables" Season 1, Episode 1** Demi studies cartoon characters through the lens of the Black American tradition of the cookout (and whether each cartoon deserves an invite).
* **"Birds, Celebrities, and Bodies" Season1, Episode 4:** Paul Robalino, who is most often known for his behind-the-camera roles writer and producer at Dropout, gives a hilarious techno-futurist recipe for improving the human body.

## Very Important People

![vip](/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/vip.jpg)

This brand-new show gives a comedian a complete makeover. They have no idea what they look like until after the makeup is done, and moments before the interview starts. They'll have to quickly come up with a character and give a totally improvised interview with host Vic Michaelis, who plays a ["straight man"](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StraightMan) journalist with varying professionalism.

Admittedly, this show is a bit of an acquired taste. The humor is a lot dryer than other Dropout shows, and the episodes are a slow burn. I love Vic Michaelis' other work and I'm glad the show has been renewed for a second season, but I won't be offended if you tell me this show didn't land for you.

* [Season 1 Episode 6: Tommy Shriggly](https://www.dropout.tv/very-important-people/season:1/videos/tommy-shriggly): My favorite episode by far, Zac Oyama plays a mental health advocate with a dark past. This is the only episode that has caused both actors to break character multiple times.
* [Season 1 Episode 5: Kelp and Dr. Milk](https://www.dropout.tv/very-important-people/season:1/videos/kepl-and-dr-milk): director and performer Josh Ruben balances playing two characters at once: a nerdy, awkward puppeteer and the charismatic precocious muppet Dr. Milk.

## Total Forgiveness

![total-forgiveness](/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/total-forgiveness.jpeg)

*Total Forgiveness* asks a simple question: to what lengths would you go to remove your college debt? Faced with the opportunity to make a life-changing amount of money, two friends challenge each other to increasingly difficult challenges to tackle their overwhelming student debt. This show is comedic, heartbreaking, and incredibly wholesome all at once.

## Dirty Laundry

<img src="/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/dirty-laundry.jpeg" alt="dirty-laundry" style="zoom:150%;" />

Another good background show, *Dirty Laundry* has guests spill their deepest secrets and most embarrassing stories. Each episode features a mixed drink and instructions on how to make it. You can pick just about any one of these episodes, but the titles might catch your eye.

* **"Who Threw Pretzels at a Couple Having Sex?" Season 3, Episode 1**: A host of Dropout cast regulars, you'll get a feel for the show with this group of troublemakers.  
* **"Who Came Out to Their High School Girlfriend via Jesus Christ" Season 3, Episode 3:** If you're a fan of drag, you'll enjoy learning your favorite queen's deep secrets. 
* **"Who had Covid-Compliant Sex" Season 2 Episode 4:** Some people will do anything for some strange.

## Um, Actually

![um-actually](/Users/robbiemckinstry/Downloads/um-actually.jpeg)

*Um, Actually* is a nerd trivia show where the host states a subtly incorrect fact about a fandom or pop culture touchstone, and the guests must determine where the error is. This is a fun show to keep on in the background.

For this show, I'd check out the titles for familiar series, like My Hero Academia or Star Wars, and pick episodes to watch based on that. Start looking through the later seasons and work your way backward. This is the longest-running show on Dropout TV, and has been fine-tuned over the years.

Starting with Season 9, Ify Nwadiwe took over as host from the series creator and College Humor head-writer Mike Trapp, and polymath content creator Brian David Gilbert took over the fact-checking booth.
